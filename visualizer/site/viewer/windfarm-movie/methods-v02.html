TorchRotor-RANS v0.2 — Model description

This project-specific model name means PyTorch + weighted actuator-disc rotor + RANS turbulence closure. It is a physical-equation solver, with no training data, neural-network weights or learning steps. It is neither a new turbulence theory nor an accuracy-validated reproduction of a paper. This windfarm uses standard k–ε; SST/LF18 is not enabled.

Limits of agreement with the paper

This is a paper-parameter transfer experiment. It cannot be described as “all parameters match the paper except the grid”: domain size, terrain, the 23-turbine layout, towers/nacelles, Reynolds number, discretization and time advancement all differ. Explicit parameters, derived values, unreported parameters and retained assumptions are listed individually in torch_rotor_rans_v02_parameter_audit.json。

The paper uses outer diameter 0.4647 m, inner diameter 0.09 m and thickness 0.08 m. This scene preserves the inner-diameter and thickness ratios for each turbine, giving an inner diameter of approximately 15.9 m and thickness approximately 14.1 m. On an 8 m grid, axial support spans only approximately 1.8 grid intervals; the kernel is sensitive to grid phase, so improved accuracy cannot be claimed. Longer runs do not automatically resolve discretization error. The paper’s printed general smoothing recommendation σ√N≳2.5Δx is also unmet here: approximately 5 m<20 m. The paper’s thin-rotor thickness and listed wind-tunnel grid exhibit this same inconsistency; thickness has not been enlarged to conceal it.

Run configuration

• All 23 existing turbines retain their hub positions, radii and fixed orientations.

• Scene ENU coordinates: x east, y north, z up; origin offset [-1776, -992, 0] m.

• Computational domain 4096 × 2048 × 512 m; 512 × 256 × 64 = 8,388,608 voxels at 8 × 8 × 8 m spacing. Of these, 5,321,913 are fluid cells; solid voxels are excluded from the fluid solve.

• Inlet along +x at 10 m/s; air density 1.225 kg/m³ and kinematic viscosity 1.5×10⁻⁵ m²/s.

• Inlet turbulence intensity I=0.3%, length scale L≈5.303 m; k=1.5(UI)²=0.00135 m²/s². I comes from the cited experiment, and L from a reference assumption scaled by rotor diameter. Neither is an explicitly stated boundary input in the paper’s B.2.

• Ct=0.95, Gaussian σ/D=(0.08/4)/0.4647≈0.04304, truncated at 2σ. Ct is a paper wind-tunnel parameter; the σ ratio is derived from the paper’s thickness and default truncation, without actual turbine calibration.

• Initialized from projected uniform inflow, with rotor force ramped linearly over the first 5 s. Trial duration 0–600 s, recorded every 5 s, giving 121 frames. The 0 s frame is the actual initialized field, not an invented sample.

• The 600 s run is a finite transient trial. Duration alone cannot establish steady state across the approximately 4 km windfarm. Final screening separately examines domain-wide velocity, turbulence quantities and per-turbine thrust over the last 100 s. Domain advection time at 10 m/s is approximately 410 s.

• GPU PyTorch float32; protocol-exported velocities are float32, while browser animation data are compressed to float16.

1. Turbine simplification

Weighted body-force actuator disc, without resolving individual blade surfaces or a rotating mesh.

Let n be the axial unit vector, xh the hub position, R the radius, s=(x−xh)·n the axial distance and r the radial distance. The weight is:

w(x) = exp[−s²/(2σ²)] when R_inner≤r≤R and |s|≤2σ; 0 elsewhere.

Radii come from existing scene geometry, with inner/outer diameter ratio 0.09/0.4647. Towers and nacelles use a separate solid mask. Solid cells are excluded from disc averaging and forcing.

Ud = |Σ(w V u) · n / Σ(w V)|
a = [1 − sqrt(1−Ct)] / 2
T = 2 ρ A a/(1−a) Ud²，A=πR²
f_rotor/ρ = −T n w / [ρ Σ(w V)]

Here Ud is the weighted mean axial velocity over the disc, and T is thrust; the fluid receives the reaction force. For Ct=.95, a≈.3882, equivalent to the disc-velocity form Ct′≈2.5380.

When cell-centred forces are interpolated to staggered velocity faces, solids block some faces. The implementation renormalizes each force component over the actual open faces so the discrete volume integral matches the target thrust, and records conservation error. Disc regions may overlap; forces add.

Excluded: blade-element momentum (BEM), airfoil lift/drag curves, resolved tip vortices, swirl/shaft torque, actual rotor-speed control, pitch control, generator efficiency, power prediction, flexible blades, platform motion, moorings and waves. Browser blade rotation uses disc velocity and an assumed tip-speed ratio of 7 solely for display.

2. Grid and geometry discretization

• Uniform orthogonal Cartesian voxel grid, without AMR, local refinement, body-fitted meshes or sliding rotating meshes.

• Pressure-projection potential, k and ε are cell-centred; velocity components occupy the corresponding cell faces in a staggered MAC layout.

• Terrain, towers and nacelles use a binary solid mask with stair-step boundaries. Normal flux across fluid/solid interfaces is zero.

• Reuses the existing 8 m geometry, conservatively aggregated from 4 m solid voxels: a coarse voxel is solid if any child voxel is solid. Existing preprocessing filled 98 under-resolved slit fluid voxels; mask provenance is recorded in the geometry configuration.

• Existing edge extension supplies computational terrain beyond source coverage, while visualization still masks areas without terrain evidence. Extended values must not be interpreted as observed terrain.

• The 8 m spacing coarsely represents towers, nacelles, slopes and rotor edges. Grid independence has not been established.

3. Governing equations

Incompressible, constant-density, isothermal RANS/URANS form, where u denotes modeled mean velocity:

∇·u = 0
∂u/∂t + ∇·(u⊗u)
  = −∇π + ∇·[(ν+νt)(∇u+∇uᵀ−⅔(∇·u)I)] + f_rotor/ρ

π is modified pressure divided by density, including the isotropic turbulent-kinetic-energy contribution. The code primarily retains pressure-projection potential, without experimentally calibrated absolute pressure.

Standard k–ε closure:

νt = Cμ k²/ε
Pk = νt ∇u : [∇u+∇uᵀ−⅔(∇·u)I]
∂k/∂t + ∇·(u k) = ∇·[(ν+νt/σk)∇k] + Pk − ε
∂ε/∂t + ∇·(u ε) = ∇·[(ν+νt/σε)∇ε]
                      + Cε1 (ε/k) Pk − Cε2 ε²/k

Constants: Cμ=.09, Cε1=1.44, Cε2=1.92, σk=1, σε=1.3. Dissipation terms for ε and k use local implicit updates; transport and production are explicit. Negative values are not silently clipped to conceal instability: nonpositive or nonfinite fluid states stop the run.

The model does not solve compressible acoustics, temperature/buoyancy, atmospheric thermal stability, pollutants or actual weather evolution.

4. Spatial, temporal and pressure algorithms

ComponentImplementation
Momentum and k, ε advectionFinite-volume fluxes with first-order upwinding; shared projected face velocities, with no transport across solid faces
DiffusionTwo-point central differences on an orthogonal grid; variable diffusivity averaged arithmetically to faces
Viscous stressSymmetric deviatoric stress including cross-velocity gradients; separate traction flux at walls
GradientsGauss differences of face values, with zero wall velocity
Time integrationExplicit prediction + pressure projection; locally implicit k, ε dissipation. This is not a line-by-line port of OpenFOAM SIMPLE/PIMPLE
TimestepSafe timestep accounts for advection, diffusion and rotor-force acceleration; transport safety factor .25, maximum .25 s, landing exactly on output times
PressurePoisson solve on the fluid mask, correcting face velocities to satisfy the discrete divergence constraint
Linear solverPreconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) + geometric multigrid V-cycle
MultigridFixed 3D neighbor convolutions and damped Jacobi smoothing; average-pooling residual restriction, nearest-neighbor prolongation, and max-pooling construction of coarse solid/fluid masks
Acceptance checksFluid divergence RMS <10⁻⁵ s⁻¹; also checks positivity, finite values, speed limits and force conservation

These convolution/pooling operations are fixed numerical operators, not trainable neural-network layers. Float32 precision affects linear residuals and mass conservation; meeting these thresholds does not establish physical accuracy.

5. Boundary conditions and wall functions

• West inlet: open fluid faces have u=(10,0,0) m/s, with fixed inlet k and ε.

• East outlet: zero pressure-projection potential, with zero-normal-gradient extrapolation for velocity and turbulence transport. Dedicated turbulence conditions for backflow inlet are not fully implemented.

• Sides and top: slip and impermeable; this does not represent an infinitely open atmospheric upper boundary.

• Terrain, towers, nacelles and bottom: impermeable, with tangential stress from k–ε wall functions approximating no-slip wall drag.

• Distance from the first fluid-cell centre to the voxel wall is h/2. κ=.41 and E=9.8; wall νt follows y+ branches, εwall=Cμ^(3/4)k^(3/2)/(κy), and wall production is estimated from velocity gradients and friction velocity.

• When a fluid cell touches multiple walls, εwall and production use an equal-area face average. This stair-step boundary extension is a project approximation; equivalence to OpenFOAM complex-wall/corner handling has not been established.

6. Browser visualization and result limitations

Exports all three velocity components at 80 m above terrain: linear interpolation between adjacent vertical cell centres, followed by explicit invalid masks for missing terrain coverage, solids and out-of-domain samples. Animation colours show Ux (velocity along the inlet direction), not speed magnitude or generated power. The browser retains a 0–20 m/s colour scale. Values below 0 and above 20 saturate at the endpoints, labeled ≤0 and ≥20. Raw fields retain negative Ux; static diagnostics use a range that includes negative values.

The timeline uses the actual 0–600 s recording; the browser linearly interpolates adjacent frames for display. Looping repeats the recording without continuing computation. Geometry retains its original coordinates, and both simulation-grid origin and display-sample centres are explicitly recorded.

A completed run, passed contract checks and a playable viewer do not establish experimental accuracy. Current wind-tunnel k–ε results have normalized-deficit RMSE of approximately .119–.134 against the paper curves, with a substantial remaining discrepancy. This error cannot be directly transferred to the full-scale terrain windfarm.

Purpose of this version: check coupling of 23 rotors, terrain and turbulence transport, and inspect startup wakes. It cannot support engineering load certification or power guarantees.

7. Recorded checks for the 600 s run

Completed 10,600 timesteps and 121 actual recorded frames. The final 500–600 s passed the predefined finite-window stability screening：

• Cumulative RMS-change upper bounds for domain-wide u, v and w were 0.002264, 0.000455 and 0.000452 m/s respectively, each below the 0.05 m/s threshold.

• Cumulative relative L2-change metrics for k and ε were 0.302% and 0.333%, each below the 2% threshold. Adjacent samples use different denominators; these cumulative metrics are change indicators, not strict upper bounds on relative endpoint error.

• The largest relative per-turbine thrust range within the window was 0.0776%, below the 1% threshold.

• RMS change in the 80 m AGL Ux slice from 500 to 600 s was 0.005589 m/s.

• Maximum recorded divergence RMS was 1.002e-06 s⁻¹; maximum recorded relative force-conservation error was 9.79e-08.

These thresholds are project engineering-screening criteria, not convergence criteria from the paper. Results support small changes over the last 100 s, without establishing asymptotic steady state, grid independence or experimental accuracy. Final mean disc velocity was approximately 7.249 m/s and total thrust approximately 10.076 MN; neither is generated power.
